        Mastering the DATEDIF Function in Excel
      

      

        The DATEDIF function is Microsoft Excel's dedicated syntax for computing elapsed intervals between two dates. Syntax format:
      


      

        =DATEDIF(start_date, end_date, unit)
      


      
      

        

          
            

              Unit Code
              Meaning
              Example Output
              Best Used For
            

          
          
            

              "Y"
              Completed full years
              25
              Standard legal age on forms
            

            

              "M"
              Total elapsed months
              302
              Infant age and loan tenures
            

            

              "D"
              Total elapsed days
              9,215

              "YM"
              Months ignoring completed years
              2

              "MD"

          ⚠️ Important Note on the "MD" Argument in Excel
        

        

          Microsoft has documented that in certain legacy builds of Excel, "MD" can occasionally return a negative number or slight discrepancy when comparing end-of-month dates (e.g. comparing January 31 to March 1). If you experience this in large datasets, use the modern substitute:
        

        

          =DATE(YEAR(TODAY()), MONTH(TODAY()), DAY(A2)) - DATE(YEAR(TODAY()), MONTH(TODAY()) - 1, DAY(A2))
        

      

    


    
    

      

        Frequently Asked Questions
      

      

        

          
            Why does Excel not show DATEDIF in formula autocomplete?
            +
          
          

            DATEDIF was originally developed by Lotus 1-2-3. When Microsoft imported it into Excel for backward compatibility, they did not include it in the formula autocomplete dropdown, but it functions in all Excel editions (including Excel Online, 2016, 2019, 2021, and Microsoft 365).
          

        


        

          
            How do I get exact decimal age like 25.5 years in Excel?
            +
          
          

            Use the =YEARFRAC(A2, TODAY(), 1) formula. The third parameter 1 specifies Actual/Actual day count convention, accounting precisely for leap years.
          

        


        

          
            How do I fix a #NUM! error in Excel age calculations?
            +
          
          

            A #NUM! error indicates that the start date in cell A2 is after the end date (e.g. a future birth date). Verify that your cell format is recognized as an actual Date by Excel, or wrap your formula in =IF(A2>TODAY(), "Future Date", DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y")).
