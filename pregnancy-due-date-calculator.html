How the Medical Due Date is Calculated (Naegele’s Rule)

The international standard for obstetric gestational age calculation is Naegele's Rule, established by German obstetrician Franz Karl Naegele. The rule adds 1 year, subtracts 3 months, and adds 7 days to the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), equating to 280 days (40 completed weeks).

1st Trimester Weeks 1 to 12. Foundations of brain, spinal cord, and all organs formed.
2nd Trimester Weeks 13 to 27. Rapid skeletal growth, fetal movement felt, audible heartbeats.
3rd Trimester Weeks 28 to 40+. Lung maturation, fat accumulation, head engaged.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is estimated due date (EDD) calculated using Naegele's rule? ↓

Naegele's rule calculates the estimated due date by adding 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), adjusting for cycle length deviations from the standard 28-day cycle.

What are the Wonder Weeks mental leaps in baby development? ↓

Wonder Weeks refers to 10 predictable neurodevelopmental leaps occurring in the first 20 months of a baby's life, during which infants develop new perceptual, spatial, and cognitive skills.

How do menstrual cycle lengths affect my due date? ↓

Standard calculations assume a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. If your cycle is longer (e.g. 32 days), ovulation happens later, extending the due date by 4 days. Shorter cycles pull the due date earlier.
