Why Age Differs Across Planets (Kepler's Third Law)

A year is defined as the time it takes an astronomical body to complete one full revolution around its star. Under Kepler's Third Law of Planetary Motion, planets closer to the Sun move faster and have shorter orbital perimeters (Mercury takes only 88 days), while distant gas giants like Neptune take over 164 Earth years to orbit once.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my age different on other planets? ↓

A year is defined as the time it takes a planet to complete one full orbit around the Sun. Because Mercury orbits the Sun in just 88 Earth days, you have more planetary birthdays there, whereas Mars takes 687 Earth days, making you roughly half your Earth age on Mars.

How is my weight calculated on different planets? ↓

Your weight depends on surface gravity. For example, Mars has 38% of Earth's surface gravity, so an individual weighing 70 kg on Earth weighs approximately 26.6 kg on Mars.

How old would a 30-year-old Earthling be on Mars? ↓

A 30-year-old on Earth would be approximately 15.9 Mars years old because one Martian orbital year equals 1.8808 Earth solar years.
